# Field Measurement Checklist

Date: 2026-07-01  
Purpose: collect real site dimensions for the front facade, canopy, terrace, stairs, AC/service constraints, drainage clues, and lighting/electrical coordination before editing detailed lighting docs or calculating purchases.

This is not a construction drawing, electrical approval, structural survey, or contractor quote. Record actual field values before ordering LED profiles, cladding, stone, pavers, cable, transformers, fixtures, or plants.

## Protected Elements

- Preserve the real house geometry.
- Do not change roof shape, roof slope, wall positions, windows, doors, column rhythm, or main proportions.
- Keep the AC unit unless the owner explicitly approves relocation/removal.
- Do not cut beams, waterproofing, roof-adjacent elements, columns, or facade surfaces without architecture/electrician/contractor review.
- Keep all exterior lighting at 2700K unless a specific exception is explicitly approved; 3000K is the absolute maximum by project rule.

## How To Measure

- Use a laser measure for long straight runs and a tape for edges, treads, risers, columns, and profiles.
- Record values in millimeters when practical; meters are fine for long runs if exact enough.
- Measure each critical length twice from different positions.
- Photograph every measured line with a visible tape/laser point if possible.
- Write the date, person measuring, weather/ground condition, and tool used.
- Do not climb roof edges or unsafe ladders for this checklist; contractor can verify elevated details.
- Electrical capacity, circuits, RCD/diff protection, and final routes must be checked by a qualified electrician.

## Priority

| Priority | Measure first | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Must-have | North/survey source, house facade length, canopy length/width, terrace length/depth, entry steps, terrace step, AC clearance, downspouts, existing 220V/service locations | Prevents wrong purchase lengths, blocked service, drainage mistakes, and rework. |
| Should-have | Column spacing, soffit/beam spacing, opening offsets, plinth heights, roof overhangs, gate/wicket clear widths | Needed for clean lighting layout, cladding details, and buildability. |
| Nice-to-have | Planting bed envelopes, gravel-strip widths, path/garden marker points, camera/view comfort notes | Helps later landscape and lighting coordination. |
| Later | Final fixture aiming, beam angles, scene tuning, exact plant counts | Do after materials, paths, planting zones, and electrical routes stabilize. |
