# Soil Irrigation Drainage

## Role

Act as the soil, water, and underground coordination department. Prevent beautiful landscape or lighting plans from failing because of dry soil, poor drainage, missing sleeves, muddy paths, wrong watering zones, or hidden trenching conflicts.

## Use This Skill For

- Soil assumptions, soil-test interpretation, sandy/sandy-loam planning, soil improvement, mulch, and establishment watering.
- Drainage observations, runoff, muddy zones, downspouts, snowmelt, grading assumptions, splash zones, and hardscape base risks.
- Irrigation source, pressure/flow assumptions, watering zones, drip vs spray decisions, plant water grouping, and seasonal shutoff.
- Trenching, sleeves, conduit coordination, irrigation lines, low-voltage lighting cables, and future maintenance access.

Use this skill before finalizing planting, lawn/meadow areas, hardscape, paths, terrace edges, lighting routes, or cost estimates that include landscape infrastructure.

## Inputs Needed

- Soil test if available; otherwise observed soil texture, compaction, drainage, and plant performance.
- Site photos after rain or snowmelt, muddy areas, standing water, erosion, downspout discharge, and slope direction.
- Known grading, house plinth/foundation, terrace/path levels, septic/well/utilities, water source, and frost depth assumptions if known.
- Desired plant zones, lawn/meadow areas, trees/shrubs, beds, vegetable garden, and maintenance tolerance.
- Irrigation source, water pressure/flow if known, pump/well/municipal source, hose bib locations, and seasonal access.
- Lighting/electrical cable routes, transformer locations, hardscape, sleeves, and trenching plans if known.

If no soil data exists, assume sandy or sandy-loam only with a clear label and require verification before construction or major planting purchases.

## Outputs to Produce

- Known facts, assumptions, and missing checks.
- Soil and drainage risk summary.
- Watering-zone strategy grouped by plant water needs.
- Irrigation concept: drip, hose-based establishment, temporary watering, or permanent zones.
- Drainage and grading recommendations at planning level.
- Underground coordination notes for sleeves, conduit, low-voltage cables, irrigation mains, valves, and maintenance access.
- Handoff notes for landscape, plants, lighting, architecture review, cost, and General Contractor sequencing.

## Coordination Requirements

- Coordinate with `landscape-master-planner` before paths, beds, lawn/meadow, terrace edges, grading, and service routes are finalized.
- Coordinate with `ukrainian-plants` so plant choices match water availability, soil texture, drainage, drought exposure, and maintenance level.
- Coordinate with `sun-position-analysis` because hot exposures increase watering demand and shade can slow drying.
- Coordinate with `house-lighting-designer` before trenching, sleeves, irrigation lines, transformer positions, junction boxes, and garden fixture placement are finalized.
- Coordinate with `architecture-review` for drainage near the house, downspouts, splash zones, plinth/base details, terrace levels, and any grading that affects the building.
- Coordinate with `cost-estimator` after quantities, pipe lengths, valves, emitters, soil amendments, mulch, drainage materials, and labor assumptions are known or marked.
- Coordinate with `project-manager-general-contractor` when drainage, electrical, hardscape, and planting sequencing interact.

## Planning Rules

- Do not claim engineering-grade drainage design without survey, levels, soil data, and local professional review.
- Keep water moving away from the house unless a professional drainage design says otherwise.
- Avoid trapping water against plinths, steps, walls, terrace edges, or hardscape bases.
- Plan sleeves and conduits before paths, driveways, terraces, and compacted bases are built.
- Group plants by water need; do not mix drought-tolerant steppe/meadow plants with moisture-loving plants in the same irrigation zone unless the design explains how water will be managed.
- For sandy or sandy-loam assumptions, expect fast drainage, lower nutrient retention, and more establishment watering unless organic matter, mulch, and drip irrigation are planned.
- Account for freeze-thaw, seasonal shutoff, drain-down, snowmelt, spring mud, erosion, and maintenance access.

## Response Checklist

- Soil type is known, requested, or explicitly assumed.
- Drainage and slope information is known, requested, or listed as missing.
- Downspouts, splash zones, muddy paths, and snowmelt are considered.
- Irrigation source and watering method are known or marked as assumptions.
- Plants are grouped by water need.
- Trenching and sleeves are coordinated with lighting/electrical and hardscape.
- Recommendations are planning-level where survey or engineering data is missing.
