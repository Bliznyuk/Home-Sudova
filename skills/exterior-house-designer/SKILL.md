# Exterior House Designer

## When to Use

Use this skill for exterior house concepts, facade palettes, entry and terrace composition, canopy refinements, wall finishes, trim, plinths, steps, and visual direction.

Pair with `architecture-review` if the idea touches structure, drainage, roof edges, columns, windows, doors, or any built geometry. Pair with `image-prompting` when the output will become a visualization prompt.

## Inputs to Ask For

- Current photos from each relevant angle.
- Marked-up view or facade zone name.
- Existing drawings or dimensions if proportions matter.
- Known materials, colors, and finishes to preserve.
- North orientation or facade orientation if sun exposure matters.
- Desired mood, references, budget level, and maintenance tolerance.
- Whether the AC unit must remain visible, be softened, or be screened.

## Output Format

Return:

1. Existing conditions observed from the provided material.
2. Protected elements that must not change.
3. Design concept in 3-6 bullets.
4. Material and color palette table.
5. Zone-by-zone recommendations.
6. Risks, assumptions, and questions.
7. Next-step prompt or implementation checklist when useful.

## Expert Rules

- Preserve real house geometry and main proportions.
- Do not move, resize, recolor, or reshape existing windows and doors unless explicitly requested.
- Do not change roof shape, roof slope, column rhythm, wall positions, or structural proportions.
- Keep the AC unit unless explicitly requested otherwise.
- Favor organic modern / warm natural minimalism.
- Prefer wood, warm greige mineral plaster, dark graphite-brown metal, natural stone, and microcement.
- Avoid glossy black-white contrast, cold showroom styling, high-tech facade language, and decorative clutter.
- Consider Ukrainian climate: freeze-thaw, snow splashback, rain, summer heat, and maintainable surfaces.
- Separate visual suggestions from construction-ready decisions.

## Checklist

- Protected geometry listed.
- Style direction remains warm, natural, and minimal.
- Materials are plausible outdoors in Ukraine.
- Base/plinth materials can handle water, mud, snow, and impact.
- Proposed wood is protected from ground contact and standing water.
- Facade changes do not conflict with drainage, gutters, roof overhangs, or AC service access.
- Any missing measurements, orientation, or material facts are requested or marked as assumptions.

## Example Triggers

- "Make the front facade warmer and more organic modern."
- "Suggest materials for the entrance facade without changing windows."
- "How can we integrate the AC unit visually?"
- "Create a warm greige and wood concept for this terrace view."
- "Review this exterior render and make it less cold."
