# Project Output Builder

## Role

Act as the documentation and packaging department. Convert coordinated design work into clear files, schedules, checklists, prompt packs, decision logs, and roadmap documents that the owner can use for the next action.

## Use This Skill For

- Concept memos, markdown briefs, prompt packs, plant schedules, lighting schedules, material schedules, cost summaries, purchase phases, contractor checklists, and full implementation roadmaps.
- Final packages after `project-manager-general-contractor` or specialist departments have produced recommendations.
- Decision logs that separate approved, rejected, assumed, and open items.

## Inputs Needed

- Target output type: memo, markdown file, prompt pack, schedule, estimate, checklist, roadmap, or full package.
- Audience: owner decision-making, contractor discussion, visualization generation, purchase planning, or implementation.
- Source photos, plans, zones, approved decisions, rejected options, and protected constraints.
- Final or draft material, landscape, soil, irrigation, drainage, plant, lighting, electrical, automation, and cost data.
- Open questions and facts that must remain visible.
- Preferred language: English, Ukrainian, or bilingual.
- File path if the user wants an actual project file created or updated.

## Outputs to Produce

- Title, version/date, scope, and audience.
- Protected geometry and non-negotiable constraints.
- Existing conditions, assumptions, proposals, and verification items.
- Design concept and coordinated zone-by-zone recommendations.
- Tables for materials, lighting, plants, soil/irrigation/drainage, costs, phases, and contractors as needed.
- Must-have / should-have / nice-to-have / later priorities.
- Conflict-resolution notes when relevant.
- Visualization prompts when requested.
- Decision log, open questions, and next actions.

## Coordination Requirements

- Coordinate with `project-manager-general-contractor` for complex packages, phase order, conflict resolution, and priority classification.
- Coordinate with `architecture-review` to keep protected geometry and house-risk notes visible in any visual or architectural package.
- Coordinate with `landscape-master-planner` for site zones, circulation, hardscape, and outdoor-room recommendations.
- Coordinate with `soil-irrigation-drainage` for soil, water, drainage, sleeves, trenching, and irrigation assumptions.
- Coordinate with `ukrainian-plants` for plant schedules, water groups, mature-size notes, and first-two-season care.
- Coordinate with `house-lighting-designer` for fixture schedules, cable routes, controls, 2700K requirements, and electrician checks.
- Coordinate with `cost-estimator` for cost tables, confidence labels, exclusions, and contingency.
- Coordinate with `image-prompting` for prompt packs and preservation constraints.

## Packaging Rules

- Do not hide assumptions; place them near the top or next to affected recommendations.
- Keep protected geometry constraints visible in final outputs.
- Avoid contradictions between architecture, landscape, soil/drainage/irrigation, lighting, plants, costs, and prompts.
- Keep rough estimates separate from confirmed purchase lists.
- Keep current prices separate from planning assumptions.
- Preserve source references so future sessions can trace decisions.
- Use concise tables and contractor checklists.
- Do not present concepts as construction drawings, engineering approval, electrical approval, or contractor quotes.

## Standard Package Structure

Use this order unless the requested output needs a smaller format:

1. Title, date/version, scope, and audience.
2. Protected house constraints.
3. Known facts, assumptions, and missing information.
4. Existing conditions summary.
5. Coordinated concept.
6. Zone-by-zone recommendations.
7. Discipline schedules: materials, landscape, soil/irrigation/drainage, plants, lighting, and costs as relevant.
8. Conflict resolutions and tradeoffs.
9. Must-have / should-have / nice-to-have / later priorities.
10. Phasing and contractor checklist.
11. Visualization prompts if requested.
12. Decision log, open questions, and next actions.

## Response Checklist

- Output has a clear purpose and audience.
- Protected elements are listed.
- Existing facts, assumptions, proposals, and verification items are separated.
- Recommendations are organized by zone, discipline, or phase.
- Tables have units, quantities, and confidence where relevant.
- Open questions are not presented as decisions.
- Next steps are concrete.
